Brazilian record labels

Three labels represented in the Acervo: two independent Rio de Janeiro labels of the 1960s and the oldest record company in Brazil.

Forma

Rio de Janeiro · from 1964 · founded by Roberto Quartin and Wadi Gebara

An independent label noted for the quality of its catalogue and design.

Founded by producer Roberto Quartin, who directed its artistic output, and architect Wadi Gebara Netto, who financed and administered it. Although short-lived, the label produced a catalogue now regarded as essential.

Its first release was Eumir Deodato's interpretation of Tom Jobim compositions. The catalogue includes Moacir Santos's Coisas, Baden Powell and Vinicius de Moraes's Os Afro-Sambas, the first recordings of Quarteto em Cy, and Cinema Novo soundtracks. Quartin sold the catalogue to PolyGram in 1969.

Key records

  • Inútil Paisagem — Eumir Deodato
  • Coisas — Moacir Santos (1965)
  • Os Afro-Sambas — Baden Powell & Vinicius de Moraes (1966)

Equipe

Rio de Janeiro · 1964–1973 · founded by Oswaldo Cadaxo

An independent label spanning instrumental samba-jazz, popular music and international pop.

Founded by Oswaldo Cadaxo, its president and producer, with Dulce Domingues as general manager; both came from Musidisc. Its early catalogue includes the samba-jazz recordings of Os Catedráticos, a studio ensemble led by Eumir Deodato.

Equipe also distributed American and British labels in Brazil and issued local versions of international hits. After its closure, Cadaxo founded the Padrão label.

Key records

  • Impulso! Samba — Os Catedráticos (1964)
  • Tremendão — Os Catedráticos (1964)
  • Ataque — Os Catedráticos (1965)

Odeon

Rio de Janeiro · since 1913 · later EMI-Odeon

The oldest name in Brazilian recording and the label of the first bossa nova record.

Fred Figner's Casa Edison, a Rio de Janeiro importer of gramophones and records, partnered with the European Odeon company to open a pressing plant in Vila Isabel in 1913, the first large-scale record factory in South America. Odeon later became part of the British group EMI, hence the EMI-Odeon name on later releases.

In 1958 it released João Gilberto's single "Chega de Saudade", arranged by Tom Jobim and widely considered the first bossa nova recording; the album followed in 1959. Odeon remained central to Brazilian music for decades, issuing works such as Milton Nascimento and Lô Borges's Clube da Esquina (1972).

Key records

  • Chega de Saudade — João Gilberto (1959)
  • Clube da Esquina — Milton Nascimento & Lô Borges (1972)
